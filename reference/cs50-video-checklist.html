Reference card

CS50x final project video

Everything the specification actually requires, on one page. Check this against the source before you record — a course page can change, this card cannot.

Hard requirements

RuleThe specification's words
Length“no more than 3 minutes in length”. Not a target — a ceiling.
OpeningMust display “your project's title; your name; your GitHub and edX usernames; your city and country; and, the date you have recorded this video.”
Body“demonstrate your project in action, as with slides, screenshots, voiceover, and/or live action.”
HostYouTube, or a similar service if YouTube is blocked where you are.
VisibilityPublic or unlisted. “don't flag it as ‘private’” — a private video cannot be graded.
READMEA file named exactly README.md containing the title, the video URL, and a detailed description. Around 750 words.
SubmitFill in the video form, then run submit50 cs50/problems/2026/x/project, then check cs50.me/cs50x.

The and/or trap. CS50x says “slides, screenshots, voiceover, and/or live action”, so a silent captioned video passes. Other CS50 courses differ: the Cybersecurity course says you “minimally need to include a voiceover”, and the on-campus College version adds “ideally with you yourself appearing”. Check the page for your course, not a remembered rule from someone else's.

The word budget

Comfortable speech is roughly 150 words per minute. That is the number that turns an abstract three-minute limit into something you can count on a page.

RuntimeSpoken words
0:30about 75 words
1:00about 150 words
2:30about 375 words — a sensible script length
3:00about 450 words — the absolute ceiling

Write to 2:30, not 3:00. You will slow down when recording, and the title card eats time without using any of your words.

A five-beat shape that fits

BeatWhat happens
0:00–0:12
Title		A still card holding the six required facts. No narration needed; read the title aloud if you like.
0:12–0:30
Problem		One sentence on what was annoying, one on what you built. No architecture yet.
0:30–2:00
Demo		The software running. The longest beat by far. Show, do not describe.
2:00–2:30
Decision		One thing that was hard and how you solved it. This is where a project stops sounding like a tutorial.
2:30–2:45
Close		Name, thanks, and where the code lives.
