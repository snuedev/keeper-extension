Script your three minutes

Before you install a single recording tool, you write the words. By the end of this page you will have a finished, timed script for Keeper — and you will know it fits, because you counted.

Why the script comes first

The instinct is to open a recorder and start talking. Almost everyone who does that records the same video six times, each take a bit worse than the last, and ends up at 4:20 with no idea what to cut.

The reason is that three minutes is short. Not "be brief" short — genuinely constrained. The specification is a ceiling, not a target:

"Create a short video (that's no more than 3 minutes in length) in which you present your project to the world."
— CS50x Final Project

A script turns that ceiling into something you can check before you have wasted an evening. Here is the conversion that makes it possible: comfortable speech runs at roughly 150 words per minute. So three minutes is about 450 words, and a sensible script — leaving room to slow down, and for a title card that costs time without spending words — is about 375 words, which is a page and a half.

The one number to remember. 150 words per minute. It is the only arithmetic in this lesson, and it converts an abstract limit into something you can count in a text box. You will use it for every talk you ever give.

A shape that reliably fits

You do not have to invent a structure. This one fits inside three minutes with room to breathe, and it puts the two things a grader is looking for — that it works, and that you understood what you built — in the places they will actually be watched.

BeatWhat it doesWords
TitleA still card with the six required facts. Costs seconds, not words.~20
ProblemWhat was annoying, and the one sentence naming what you built.~45
DemoKeeper running. The longest beat, and the one that earns the grade.~220
DecisionOne hard thing and how you solved it.~70
CloseThanks, and where the code lives.~20

The demo beat is deliberately more than half the script. The commonest failure in a student project video is spending two minutes describing an architecture and thirty seconds showing the software. Grading is on the project, and the video is the only way the grader sees it working.

Show, don't describe. "Notes save automatically" is a claim. Typing a word, pausing, and letting the footer change to Saved on camera is a demonstration. Wherever your script explains a feature, ask whether the screen could just show it instead — and cut the sentence if it can.

Your title card

This beat is not creative work, it is a compliance checklist, and it is the easiest thing in the whole submission to get wrong. The specification requires the opening to display:

"your project's title; your name; your GitHub and edX usernames; your city and country; and, the date you have recorded this video."

Six facts. Fill them in now, while you are thinking about it — two of them you will have to go and look up, and you do not want to be doing that at 11pm with a recorder running.

Exercise 1 — your title card

Write the script

Below is a skeleton with the beats marked and prompts where your words go. Replace the bracketed prompts with real sentences, out loud as you write — if a line is hard to say, it is the wrong line. The meter underneath counts as you type and tells you whether you still fit.

You have an advantage most students do not: your README already explains the reasoning behind every design choice in the project. The draft-rescue paragraph in particular is a ready-made "Decision" beat — a Chrome popup is destroyed the moment it loses focus, and you built around that. Do not invent new explanations; steal your own.

Exercise 2 — draft, and watch the clock

Why the meter stops at 2:45, not 3:00. Two reasons. The title card burns ten to fifteen seconds of runtime without using any of your words. And everyone reads faster in their chair than they speak on camera. A script that measures 3:00 flat becomes a video that is over the limit.

Check it stuck

Three questions, closed book. Getting one wrong is more useful than getting all three right — it shows you exactly which bit did not land.

Your script times at 2:55 and the limit is 3:00. Which beat do you cut?
